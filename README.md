# Comm-Log Send Reconciliation — `target_base` = 22

**Merchant 501 · October 2026 · Diwali campaigns · `communication_type = '2'`**

Finance reports a `target_base` of **22**. A naive `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM
communication_log` returns **30**. This repo reconciles the 8-send gap and
shows the investigation that produced it.

```bash
sqlite3 -box data/comm_log.db < sql/03_target_base.sql   # -> 22
```

---

## TL;DR

Two adjustments account for the entire gap — plus a third instinct you must
deliberately **not** act on:

| | |
|---|---|
| **−4** | Campaign `9004` is `processing_status = 'processed'` but still `approval_awaiting`. Its four sends sit in the log without having cleared sign-off. |
| **−4** | Retry chains collapse to distinct customers. `9001→9002→9003` is one underlying communication (13 rows → 10 customers, **−3**); `9201→9202` is another (6 → 5, **−1**). A customer who needed three attempts counts once. |
| **±0** | Campaign `9101` is **standalone**, so it is *not* deduped. `C20` was legitimately re-targeted on 10-Oct and 20-Oct — two events, not one. |

`30 − 4 − 4 = 22`

That last row is the whole exercise. It contributes nothing to the arithmetic,
which is exactly why it is easy to miss: every dedupe instinct says "collapse
the duplicate customer", and the one line in the data dictionary that says
otherwise is worth a full send. Dedupe `9101` along with everything else and
you land on **21** — a plausible-looking answer with no obvious error in it.

*(The bridge below reaches 22 by a different route, because that is the order I
actually found things: it over-deduped to 21 at step 5 and corrected at step 6.
Same destination, and the wrong turn is kept on purpose.)*

## The reconciliation bridge

Generated by [`sql/02_bridge.sql`](sql/02_bridge.sql) — the steps below are in
the order I discovered I needed them, wrong turns included.

| Step | Description | Result | Reason |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 0 | Naive `COUNT(*)` over `communication_log` | **30** | Starting point: assume one log row = one send. |
| 1 | Apply stated scope — merchant 501, Oct-2026, type `'2'` | **30** | **±0.** Verified rather than assumed. The whole extract is already in scope, so scope is not the gap. |
| 2 | Drop campaigns that have not cleared approval (`9004`) | **26** | **−4.** Eligibility needs a finalized `creation_status` **and** `processing_status = 'processed'`. `9004` is processed but `approval_awaiting` — the send pipeline ran ahead of approval bookkeeping. |
| 3 | Dedupe customers per **campaign** (`DISTINCT communication_id, customer_id`) | **25** | **−1.** First dedupe attempt. Wrong model: it treats each retry campaign as its own communication, so a retried customer still counts once per attempt. |
| 4 | Collapse retries one level — dedupe per `COALESCE(parent_id, id)` | **22** | **−3.** Matches Finance. I did not stop here — see step 5. |
| 5 | Resolve chains **recursively** to the true root (`9003→9002→9001`) | **21** | **−1, and it breaks the match.** But it is the correct fix: one level of `parent_id` leaves `9003` in its own bucket, double-counting `C3`. Losing 22 here is what exposed the *second* error. |
| 6 | Stop deduping **standalone** campaigns — count their rows (`9101`) | **22** | **+1.** A standalone campaign is not a chain; every send is its own event. `C20` counts twice. Restores 22 for the right reason. |
| **final** | `target_base` — [`sql/03_target_base.sql`](sql/03_target_base.sql) | **22** ✅ | Matches Finance. |

### Why step 4 is the trap

Step 4 returns the right answer from a query with **two compensating bugs**:

```
        chain 9001  →  10   ✅
        chain 9002  →   1   ❌  +1  (9003's grandparent link missed, C3 counted twice)
             9101   →   6   ❌  −1  (standalone wrongly deduped, C20 collapsed)
        chain 9201  →   5   ✅
                      ────
                        22       right total, wrong for two reasons
```

Fixing either bug alone gives 21 or 23. Fixing both gives 22. **A bridge that
stops at step 4 lands on 22 without a single correct adjustment** — exactly the
red flag the brief warns about, which is why steps 5 and 6 are shown separately
rather than merged.

### Where the 22 actually sits

From [`sql/04_breakdown.sql`](sql/04_breakdown.sql):

| Underlying communication | Rule applied | Raw rows | Distinct customers | Contributes |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `9001` (+ `9002`, `9003`) | retry chain → distinct customers | 13 | 10 | **10** |
| `9101` | standalone → count rows | 7 | 6 | **7** |
| `9201` (+ `9202`) | retry chain → distinct customers | 6 | 5 | **5** |
| **Total** | | **26** | *21* | **22** |

Note the middle column: blanket-deduping everything gives **21**. The correct
answer is neither "all rows" (26) nor "all distinct" (21) — it is row-counting
for standalones and distinct-counting for chains.

## Proving the query, not just the number

Three different queries return 22 here — the correct one, the one-level dedupe
from step 4, and a plain `COUNT(*) WHERE delivery_status = 900`. Matching the
number proves nothing on its own, so
[`tests/mutation_tests.py`](tests/mutation_tests.py) perturbs the data in ways
that force the models apart and checks mine against hand-computed expectations.

```
$ python3 tests/mutation_tests.py

  scenario                                                 expect    MINE  1-LEVEL  DELIV
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  baseline - untouched dataset                                 22      22       22     22
  chain deepens to 4 levels (9005 under 9003), re-sends C3     22      22     23 X   23 X
  standalone 9101 re-targets C20 a third time                  23      23     22 X     23
  C3 never gets delivered (all 3 attempts fail)                22      22       22   21 X
  C1 delivered twice inside chain 9001                         22      22       22   23 X
  campaign 9004 finally clears approval                        26      26       26     26
  9004 clears approval, having also re-sent C2                 26      26       26   27 X
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MINE failed 0 of 7 cases.
  The two rival models were wrong in 5 of 7 cases,
  yet all three return 22 on the untouched data.
  RESULT: PASS -- the final query holds up where the lucky ones break.
```

The rival models are wrong in 5 of 7 scenarios yet agree on the untouched data.

## What surprised me

Three things, only one of which changed the number.

**The duplicate that was supposed to stay.** Every reconciliation instinct
says a customer appearing twice under one campaign is double-counting. Here
`C20` under `9101` is real — the campaign is standalone, so the two sends are
genuinely two events. The rule inverts based on whether the campaign sits in a
retry chain, which means *you cannot dedupe `communication_log` without first
reading `campaign.parent_id`*. The dedupe key is not a property of the log row.

**`processed` does not mean countable.** `9004` has four delivered sends
(`delivery_status = 900`), real timestamps, and consumed credits — it looks
completely legitimate in the log. It fails only on `creation_status =
'approval_awaiting'`. Billing and reporting can diverge here: those four sends
burned credits but must not appear in `target_base`. That is a reconciliation
risk worth flagging to Finance, not just a filter.

**Delivery status is a decoy that happens to be right.** `COUNT(*) WHERE
delivery_status = 900` also returns 22, which nearly convinced me the metric
was "successful deliveries". It is a coincidence of this dataset: no chain
customer failed every attempt, none was delivered twice within a chain, and
every standalone row succeeded. Break any one of those and the two definitions
diverge (mutation tests 4 and 5 do exactly that). `target_base` counts
customers *targeted* by a communication — a customer who failed all three
attempts still counts.

Smaller notes: `sent_time == scheduled_time` on all 30 rows, so nothing can be
learned about send lag; `credit_used` is `1` everywhere, so credits and sends
are interchangeable *in this extract* but would not be in general; and the
retry chains are pure `parent_id` links with no self-references or cycles,
which is what makes the recursive walk safe.
